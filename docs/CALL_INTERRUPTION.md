# Bells putting phone calls on hold

## Findings and limits

The reported sequence (bell arrives, desk call goes on hold, remote caller hears music)
matches receiver-side multicast barge-in. The likely music source is the hosted PBX's
music-on-hold service, not a second audio stream generated by the bell sender. Confirm
this with a controlled call; no current handset export or SIP trace accompanied the report.

The architecture explains where the fix belongs:

- `bell/paging.py` arbitrates concurrent **Pi transmissions**. Event `priority` and
  `busy_policy: skip` do not represent phone call state.
- `bell/delivery.py` passes the zone channel to the multicast transmitter; it does not
  pass event priority into the multicast wire format. Multicast has no per-phone busy reply.
- `bell/transmit.py` and `bell/wire/poly_group_page.py` transmit calibrated audio and
  session packets. A receiver decides whether to interrupt its local SIP call.
- Phone provisioning was documented for subscriptions and codecs, but lacked an explicit
  active-call policy and a witnessed busy-phone acceptance test. That is the deployment gap
  addressed here. The actual affected phones' effective settings remain to be verified.

Yealink documents `multicast.receive_priority.priority = 0` as disabling reception of
multicast pages during an active voice call. Nonzero values allow eligible listening
priorities to interrupt. The separate `multicast.receive_priority.enable` setting controls
page-versus-page arbitration; changing it is not the active-call fix. These parameters are
documented in [Yealink's provisioning guide](https://support-cdn.yealink.com/attachment/upload/attachment/2015-3-12/3/a20b8122-2868-4c02-b61f-84338934c421/Yealink_SIP-T2_Series_IP_Phones_Auto_Provisioning_Guide_V72_30.pdf)
and [Yealink's later parameter description](https://support.yealink.com/forward2filesystem/attachment/upload/attachment/2020-7-6/3/b3798572-9489-4ee6-b612-40703c61af68/Yealink%2BSkype%2Bfor%2BBusiness%2BHD%2BIP%2BPhones%2BAdministrator%2BGuide%2BV9.16.pdf).
These references establish the parameter semantics, not certification of the installed
T31P firmware. Verify its **Directory → Multicast IP → Paging Barge** setting and behavior.
Do not confuse listening-list priority with the Poly group number.

There is also a channel-policy risk: the sample uses group 24 for routine outdoor bells
and group 25 for all-call. [Poly's CCX guide](https://kaas.hpcloud.hp.com/pdf-public/pdf_9138810_en-US-1.pdf)
assigns interrupting Priority and Emergency behavior to those groups on that receiver family.
This does not prove Yealink or Algo behaves identically. Test each configured channel on
the actual firmware. Do not renumber the site's calibrated channels as a software-only fix;
any migration requires coordinated subscriptions and new capture/acceptance evidence.

## Receiver provisioning fix

Use [the office override](../deploy/yealink/office-no-barge.cfg) for selected office phones:

```ini
multicast.receive_priority.priority = 0
```

Merge this single setting into the hosted provider's authoritative per-phone configuration.
Do not replace the full configuration with the fragment. Preserve accounts, listening
addresses, channels, codec, DND rules, and page-versus-page settings. A local web change can
serve as a pilot, but provider reprovisioning may undo it. Installing a bell software release
does not provision any phones; this PR intentionally contains no automatic handset writer.

**Emergency coverage is a rollout prerequisite.** This setting can suppress emergency
multicast pages on a busy office phone too. Do not assume event priority 100 bypasses it.
School leadership and the phone provider must establish and witness the required alert path
for occupied offices (for example, a dedicated paging speaker with suitable indoor coverage).
The outdoor horn alone does not establish indoor coverage. If the school requires emergency
barge-in on those phones, the provider must design and test selective listening priorities
or a separate supported alert path before rolling out this blanket no-barge profile.

## Pilot, verify, persist, and roll back

1. Record one affected phone's model, firmware, effective Paging Barge setting, subscriptions,
   and current provider override. Keep credential-bearing exports private and out of Git.
   The [read-only inspector](YEALINK_FLEET.md) can collect multicast web evidence; inspect the
   actual loaded value, not JavaScript defaults or a similarly named priority control.
2. With staff notified, reproduce one routine bell during a test call to an external test
   number. Record channel and whether the remote party hears hold music. Use the ordinary
   Ring Now confirmation and safety guards. A packet-send success is insufficient evidence.
3. Apply the single override to the pilot phone through its provider or web interface.
   Re-read the effective setting. Optionally audit a decrypted text CFG export locally:

   ```console
   python scripts/check_yealink_barge.py /private/phone-export.cfg
   ```

   Exit 0 means this file explicitly disables barge-in; 1 means enabled; 2 means unknown,
   unsupported, conflicting, or unreadable. Missing values never count as disabled. This
   checks a file, not provider merge precedence or effective phone behavior, and prints no
   unrelated settings. Do not submit credential-bearing exports with bug reports.
4. Witness this matrix for every routine channel used at the site (defaults 23, 24, 25):

   | Receiver state | Required result |
   |---|---|
   | Office phone idle | Bell plays normally |
   | Office phone in incoming external call | Conversation stays connected; no hold music |
   | Office phone in outgoing external call | Conversation stays connected; no hold music |
   | Other idle subscribed phones | Bell still plays |
   | Algo horn, outdoor/all-call zones | Horn still plays as configured |
   | Office phone idle after test call ends | Next bell plays normally |

5. Witness the approved emergency path with office phones both idle and in calls. Record
   what actually played and where. Do not mark acceptance complete if required alert coverage
   is lost. Use Commissioning notes to record the call tests alongside audible zone results.
6. Persist the override with the provider, reprovision/reboot the pilot during the maintenance
   window, re-read the effective setting, and repeat the matrix. Only then extend to the
   affected office phones and retain the evidence. Recheck after firmware/provider changes.
7. If reception or emergency coverage regresses, restore the captured previous setting in
   both provider configuration and phone, reprovision, and retest. Do not guess a rollback
   threshold. Restoration can reintroduce the original call interruptions.

If interruptions persist with effective Paging Barge disabled, capture a controlled call's
SIP hold transition through the provider and confirm whether the bell actually uses multicast
or a configured SIP paging destination. Check group-specific behavior and other paging
sources. Changing codec, audio volume, bell timing, or PBX hold music does not fix a receiver
that continues putting calls on hold.
